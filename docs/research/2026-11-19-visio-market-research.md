# Visio market research

**Date:** 2026-11-19.
**Question:** is there a market for a passive, all-day wearable camera that turns the day into a short AI-edited reel, and what do the numbers look like as a business?
**Method:** desk research from public web sources.

Labels: **[V]** verified against the linked source, **[R]** reported secondhand or estimated by a third party, **[E]** our own estimate.
Treat [R] and [E] numbers as directional, not as facts to plan around.

## Summary

There is a real but niche market.
Audio-only AI wearables are proven businesses (Plaud), while every earlier passive video or still lifelogger failed, and glasses are the only camera wearable to break through.
What changed is that multimodal models can now do the curation whose absence killed Google Clips, which is exactly Visio's daily reel.
The business only works if the device filters footage before it reaches the model: sending all-day video to a model costs more than any subscription can recover.
The most promising niches are parents filming their own kids at home, dementia and caregiving memory aids, and short travel or event sessions.
The credible path is an open-source DIY kit first, a small prosumer pilot second, and eldercare B2B as the most defensible revenue.

## 3. Why video lifelogging failed, and what changed

Why it failed:

- Most passive footage is boring, and nothing curated it (Google Clips).
- Bystanders pushed back.
- Battery life and heat.
- Thin hardware margins and dependence on venture funding (Narrative).

What changed:

- Multimodal models can now curate footage cheaply enough to consider. [R] [Google AI developer forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-live-2-5-token-counting-what-is-the-expected-cost-of-long-running-video-session/99245)
- Smart glasses have made being filmed in public more normal.

What has not changed:

- EU regulators are now targeting camera wearables: Hamburg's data protection authority says Meta glasses "generally" may not record people beyond close family and friends, and the EDPB says makers cannot hand their GDPR duties to the wearer. [R] [PPC Land 2026](https://ppc.land/hamburg-regulator-finds-ray-ban-meta-glasses-expose-bystanders-without-consent/)
- Some UK courts and venues have banned smart glasses. [R] [PetaPixel 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/08/04/meta-smart-glasses-face-calls-for-bans-across-europe-over-privacy-concerns/)

## 4. Unit economics [E unless linked]

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Retail price | $299-349 at today's ~$110 hobby BOM; about $199 with a custom board at 10k+ units (BOM roughly $45-65), in line with Looki and Limitless |
| Storage, 30 days of raw video | 14 GB/day x 30 days = about 420 GB per user: about $6.30/month on Cloudflare R2 at $0.015/GB-month ([R] [mecanik](https://mecanik.dev/en/posts/cloudflare-r2-pricing-explained-real-costs-vs-s3-and-backblaze/)), about $2.50 on Backblaze B2 |
| Storage, 7 days raw plus 30 days of highlights | About $1.50/month |
| Model inference, naive | 12 h/day at 1 fps with audio: about $45/month on Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, about $140/month on Gemini 2.5 Flash ([R] [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/ai/gemini-2-5-pro-and-flash-are-generally-available-and-gemini-2-5-flash-lite-preview-is-announced/)); not viable |
| Model inference, filtered | On-device or edge filtering (motion and scene change, audio energy, flag-button windows) sends about 1 h/day of candidates through batch mode: about $2-4/month |
| Total cloud cost | About $5-8 per user per month with filtering |
| Subscription for margin | $14.99-19.99/month for roughly 55-70% gross margin |

Competitors charge $0-20 a month, but they process audio, which is 10-50x cheaper than video, so Visio would sit at the top of that range and has to earn it.

The current pipeline's own cost goal (under $0.75 per recorded day in the v1 PRD) is consistent with the filtered estimate only if raw retention stays short; storage at rest is the line to watch.

## 5. Key risks

- **Recording consent:** 11-13 US states require all-party consent for audio, California allows $5k per violation, and voiceprint suits under Illinois BIPA have hit Fireflies and Otter. An always-on device also records conversations the wearer is not part of. [R] [layer3labs 2026](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/two-party-consent-states), [memx](https://memx.app/blog/ai-wearable-recording-consent-law/)
- **GDPR:** effectively rules out the EU at launch; Limitless withdrew from the EU and UK.
- **Bans:** workplaces, schools and venues limit where the device can be worn.
- **Competition:** Meta and Amazon bought the two pendant leaders, Apple is rumoured to have a camera pendant, and glasses already shoot point-of-view video.
- **Hardware:** Humane, Rabbit, Narrative and Snap show the cost of inventory and returns, and heat and battery are hard limits on a Pi Zero 2W running all day.

## 6. Bottom line

Is there a market: yes, but niche.
General-consumer "AI memory" is now a contest between platforms that a solo builder should not enter.
Demand is proven for audio, Looki is testing camera lifelogging, and passive video for everyone has failed every time.

Most promising niches [E]:

1. **Parents filming their own kids at home.** Everyone recorded is family, which eases consent, and a daily reel has strong emotional value.
2. **Dementia and caregiving memory aid.** Backed by the SenseCam research, with buyers willing to pay and controlled settings.
3. **Travel and event modes.** Short sessions avoid both the all-day cost and most of the privacy friction.

Weakest: general all-day wear, and creators, who already use a GoPro or a phone.

Credible path [E]:

1. **Open-source DIY kit** with a self-hosted or bring-your-own-API-key pipeline: no cloud cost, no liability, and a community, as Omi built with its developer kit.
2. **Prosumer pilot** at $199-249 plus about $15/month, if the kit gets traction, with the recording LED that cannot be disabled, a hard privacy control and on-device filtering.
3. **Eldercare B2B** as the most defensible revenue, which needs consent workflows and health-data handling.

The realistic exit is an acqui-hire or licensing deal, as with Limitless and Bee, rather than standalone scale.

## Implications for the current design

- On-device filtering (the flag button plus cheap motion and scene-change checks) decides whether the business works, because model cost, not storage, dominates.
- The privacy work in the v1 PRD (an LED that cannot be disabled, every boot comes up stopped, a one-gesture stop) matches what regulators now expect and carries straight over to a product.
- The 30-day raw retention decision (D6) is the main storage cost lever; 7 days of raw footage plus long-lived highlights would cut storage to roughly a quarter.
