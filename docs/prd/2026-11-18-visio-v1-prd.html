Product requirements - v1 - 2026-11-18

Visio pendant v1

Source of truth for the how: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-04-visio-pendant-design.md. This doc owns the who, the why, and what "done" means.

Problem. The best moments of an ordinary day go unrecorded because filming means stopping to hold a phone, and reviewing hours of footage nobody wants to do.

Product. A cheap pendant you wear through the day and switch on for the moments you choose: a 2 s hold starts and stops recording, a short press flags the best bit, clips upload over home Wi-Fi in the background, and a 60-120 second highlight reel of those moments lands on your phone each morning with zero editing.

Users

The only v1 user: you

Wears it around home and nearby and records the moments worth keeping, typically 1-2 hours a day rather than the whole day. Owns the Pi, the Supabase project and the repo. Being technical is not an excuse for friction: setup should be one CLI command plus app onboarding, with no hand-edited config or SSH needed day to day.

Out of scope: DIY makers

No public setup guide or kit in v1. The seamless-setup bar above keeps that door open later.

Bystanders

Family and visitors who are recorded. Not users, but a privacy constraint: the device must show clearly when it is capturing (LED), the LED cannot be turned off or dimmed to dark by config, and the wearer can stop recording with one gesture.

Goals and success metrics

1 reel / day

A reel lands every morning a day was recorded, with no manual step.

1 day / charge

A typical day of moments on one charge: about 2 h recorded, Stopped the rest of a 14 h day. Continuous recording (about 7.4 h on paper) is the stress case, not the target. Standby draw is unmeasured; see the open questions. Measured at M3 (v2); v1 runs on USB power.

~3.6 GB / day

Raw footage for a typical day of moments: 2 h at 4 Mbps, about 1.8 GB per recorded hour. Under the 7-day raw window (D6) that is about 25 GB at rest per wearer, plus kept clips and reels.

< $110

Hardware BOM.

< $0.20

Cloud cost per typical day of moments (2 h recorded): about $0.05 transcription and $0.06-0.13 vision scoring, plus storage at rest under the 7-day raw window (D6). An 8 h day stays under $0.75. Not yet checked against current Supabase pricing; see the storage risk.

95% in 24 h

Share of recorded minutes that reach cloud storage within 24 hours, from segments versus the device's own capture log.

1 command

From a freshly flashed SD card to recording: one CLI run, then QR onboarding from the app. No SSH after that, including when Wi-Fi changes.

"Worth watching"

Qualitative: on at least 4 of 5 test days, you would rather watch the reel than skip it.

Non-goals for v1

  • On-device AI inference
  • Live streaming
  • Waterproof or rugged enclosure
  • Multi-user or social features beyond export and share
  • App store release
  • Public DIY kit or setup guide

v2

  • J10 teach the reel (always / never include)
  • J11 regenerate a reel
  • Battery and enclosure (wearable all day, see M3)
  • Accelerometer tap instead of a flag button
  • Wake-word auto-flag
  • BLE status on the local network
  • Off-the-shelf pendant shell instead of a 3D print

LED: device status at all times

Requirement: whenever the pendant is running, the LED is lit and shows exactly one state, and a wearer or bystander can tell from it whether the camera is capturing. A dark LED means no power, never "recording quietly". Green, yellow and flashing red mean capturing; white, cyan and solid red mean not capturing.

This PRD owns what each state means to a person. The state machine (two regions, transitions, gestures, priorities) lives in the design spec, docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-04-visio-pendant-design.md, under "LED state machine", which is the single owner of the LED table for #100, #101 and #103, and is rendered in docs/ARCHITECTURE.html.

How pairing works today (as built)

1. Phone shows a QR

Device tab, Re-onboard: you type the Wi-Fi name and password. The QR holds those plus your login tokens.

2. Pendant reads it

Its camera decodes the QR, saves the Wi-Fi profile and your login (session.json), and joins the network.

3. Pendant registers

It adds itself to devices under your account in Supabase.

4. App finds it

The app reads your devices from Supabase and follows device_status live.

The phone and pendant never talk directly. They are "paired" only by sharing your account in Supabase, and the app knows nothing until the pendant is online. There is no Bluetooth anywhere in the code today.

Risks

First hardware contact

Every past software-done milestone hit 1-2 gaps on a real flow. Expect the same in M1; budget a fix round.

Reel quality

Scoring weights were tuned without real footage. A bench camera pointed at a desk will make dull reels; test by wearing or carrying it.

Privacy and consent

Recording others in the home, even only in moments. An LED is a signal, not consent: about a dozen US states require every party's consent to record a conversation, so audio (J8) needs a house rule, and Stopped (J14) must be one gesture away. Footage sits in Supabase storage and, until uploaded, unencrypted on the SD card next to your login tokens, so a lost pendant needs a "revoke device" path (sign out its session) and a way to delete a day on request. The LED cannot be disabled by config.

Storage is the dominant cost

At 4 Mbps a recorded hour is about 1.8 GB. A typical day of moments (assumed 2 h) is about 3.6 GB, so storage grows by roughly 110 GB a month while nothing is deleted (the cleanup step is not built). With the 7-day raw window (D6) it settles at about 25 GB per wearer plus kept clips and reels; a wearer who records all day (8 h, 14.4 GB) would settle at about 100 GB, past Supabase's included storage (check current pricing). The levers are recording moments rather than whole days, the raw window, and a lower bitrate. A session left running by mistake records until the card fills; see the open questions.

Thermals and power

Continuous encode plus Wi-Fi upload on a Zero 2W is unproven over long sessions, and the 7.4 h continuous figure is a paper estimate. Stopped still runs the Pi (about 1.2 W on paper), so a 2 h recording plus 12 h standby day needs about 17 Wh against the 11.1 Wh cell in the spec.

Decisions (resolved 2026-11-18, updated 2026-11-19)

  • v1 is done at M2: button, LED and mic working. Battery and enclosure (M3) are v2.
  • v1 is for you only, but setup must still be seamless: one CLI command plus app onboarding.
  • J10 (teach the reel) is v2, and the long-press stays in the app as is.
  • J11 (regenerate) is v2.
  • J14 (stop and start) is P0 for M2: a 2 s hold toggles, the LED is dim white while stopped, and every boot comes up stopped (recording-control spec, accepted 2026-11-18). This replaces the earlier double-press pause with a dark LED, so a dark LED only ever means no power. The app shows the state but cannot control it, because the pendant is offline most of the day.
  • One hold ladder on the single button: under 2 s flags, 2 s stops or starts, 5 s powers off safely from any state, 10 s (from Stopped) re-onboards; the LED previews each step and every gesture except the stop commits on release.
  • J12 (change Wi-Fi) is P0: manual only: a 10 s hold on the button from Stopped (M2), with an SSH command as the bench fallback, plus the QR code from the app. No automatic trigger, because the pendant is away from Wi-Fi most of the day, and scanning would also take the camera away from recording. Bluetooth is v2 (D5): it needs an Expo dev build and a GATT service on the Pi, while the QR path already exists and also carries the login session.
  • Raw footage is kept 7 days by default (D6, revised 2026-11-19 from 30), adjustable to 30; flagged and "always include" clips, and every reel, are kept forever. Like cloud cameras, continuous footage gets the shortest window, deletion is two-step with a 3-day warning, and the pendant never deletes footage that has not been backed up.
  • Record moments, not whole days (2026-11-19): storage is the dominant cost, and the wearer records the moments they choose, not 8 hours of their day. Goals, storage and cost are now sized for a typical day of about 2 h recorded (1-2 h expected). Recording all day still works, at about four times the storage, cost and power.

Open questions

  • Auto-stop a long session? Should a recording session stop itself after N minutes to protect storage, since a forgotten one records until the SD card fills? If so, what N, and how does the LED or app warn first?
  • Standby power. Is Stopped cheap enough for a day of moments on one charge, or does v2 need a lower-power standby (camera off, CPU throttled) or a smaller daily target? Measure at M3.
  • A day with very little footage. What should the nightly reel do when the day holds less than 60 s, or only one short moment: a shorter reel, no reel, or merge into the next day?
